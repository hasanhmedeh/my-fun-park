9.Health and safety

Photosensitive epilepsy warning. A very small number of people may have seizures when exposed to certain light patterns or flashing lights, including in video games, even if they have no history of epilepsy. The Funfair includes blinking and bright lights, quick changes between light and dark (for example in tunnels), fast motion and screen effects that simulate high G-forces (the screen darkening or turning red). If you or anyone in your family has epilepsy or has had seizures, talk to a doctor before playing. Stop playing immediately and seek medical advice if you feel dizzy, have blurred vision, eye or muscle twitches, disorientation, involuntary movements or loss of awareness.

Motion sickness. The rides simulate rollercoasters, drops, loops, spins and fast driving from a first-person view. This can cause motion sickness, nausea, dizziness or headaches. If you start to feel unwell, stop and rest until you feel better. Switching to a third-person camera, lowering the graphics setting or playing on a smaller screen can help.

  • Take a break of at least 10 to 15 minutes every hour, play in a well-lit room, and sit at a comfortable distance from the screen.
  • Do not play while driving, walking in traffic or doing anything else that needs your attention.
  • The physics, speeds and forces in the game are simulations for entertainment. They are not engineering or safety information about real rides.
  • Parents and guardians should supervise younger players.
